General Pathology: True or False Questions

Med school general pathology as true/false statements, each with a short explanation. Decide first, then tap a statement to check.

General pathology has many pairs that are easy to mix up: hypertrophy and hyperplasia, reversible and irreversible injury, necrosis and apoptosis, transudate and exudate, benign and malignant. True/false statements are a fast way to check that you really know which is which.

The questions follow the usual order of a general pathology course: cell adaptation, injury and death, acute and chronic inflammation, tissue repair, hemodynamic disorders, immunopathology and neoplasia.

25 practice questions

These are 25 of the 100 questions in the app’s example deck, spread across all topics.

  1. Hypertrophy is an enlargement of an organ caused by an increase in the number of cells.

    X · False Hypertrophy is an increase in cell size. An increase in cell number is hyperplasia.

  2. Cardiac muscle cells have almost no capacity to divide, so they adapt to pressure overload mainly by hypertrophy.

    O · True Pressure overload from hypertension or aortic stenosis → cardiomyocyte hypertrophy (left ventricular hypertrophy).

  3. Pyknosis, karyorrhexis and karyolysis are features of reversible cell injury.

    X · False These three nuclear changes are features of irreversible injury, i.e. necrosis.

  4. In ischemia, reduced ATP impairs the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, so sodium and water enter the cell.

    O · True The result is cellular swelling, dilation of the endoplasmic reticulum and loss of microvilli.

  5. In the intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway of apoptosis, mitochondrial outer membrane permeability increases and cytochrome c is released into the cytoplasm.

    O · True Cytochrome c forms the apoptosome with Apaf-1 and activates caspase-9.

  6. BCL-2 is a protein that promotes apoptosis.

    X · False BCL-2 and BCL-XL inhibit apoptosis. BAX and BAK promote it. BCL-2 is overexpressed in follicular lymphoma with t(14;18).

  7. When blood flow is restored after ischemia (reperfusion), injury never progresses further; reperfusion always reduces tissue damage.

    X · False Ischemia–reperfusion injury: reactive oxygen species, influx of inflammatory cells, complement activation and calcium overload during reperfusion can actually increase the damage.

  8. The most common mechanism of increased vascular permeability in acute inflammation is contraction of endothelial cells in postcapillary venules.

    O · True It is caused by histamine, bradykinin and leukotrienes, starts quickly and is short-lived (immediate transient response).

  9. Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 (LAD-1) is caused by a defect in selectin ligands.

    X · False LAD-1 is a defect in β2 integrins (CD18). Defective fucosylation of the selectin ligand (sialyl-Lewis X) is LAD-2.

  10. The main leukotriene that causes bronchoconstriction is LTB4.

    X · False LTB4 is a neutrophil chemotactic agent. Intense bronchoconstriction, vasoconstriction and increased permeability are caused by LTC4, LTD4 and LTE4.

  11. Complement C5a is a powerful chemotactic agent for neutrophils.

    O · True C5a (and C3a) are anaphylatoxins that also trigger mast cell degranulation.

  12. An ulcer is a local defect of an epithelial surface; peptic ulcers of the stomach and duodenum are classic examples.

    O · True It forms when necrotic tissue is shed. Acute and chronic inflammation are often seen together.

  13. The main infiltrating cell of chronic inflammation is the neutrophil.

    X · False The main cells of chronic inflammation are mononuclear cells such as lymphocytes, macrophages and plasma cells, with tissue destruction and fibrosis.

  14. Granulation tissue is another term for granuloma.

    X · False Granulation tissue is the tissue of new blood vessels and fibroblasts formed during wound healing. A granuloma is a pattern of chronic inflammation — something entirely different.

  15. The most important growth factor driving angiogenesis in wound healing is VEGF.

    O · True FGF-2 also takes part in angiogenesis. TGF-β is the most important factor in fibrosis.

  16. A keloid is an excessive scar that stays within the boundaries of the original wound.

    X · False A keloid grows beyond the boundaries of the original wound and rarely regresses. A scar that stays within the boundaries is a hypertrophic scar.

  17. A liver with chronic passive congestion shows centrilobular congestion with relative sparing of periportal areas, giving a "nutmeg liver" appearance.

    O · True It occurs mainly in right-sided heart failure; in severe cases centrilobular necrosis and fibrosis develop.

  18. Most pulmonary emboli arise from deep vein thrombi of the legs.

    O · True Thrombi above the popliteal vein (femoral and iliac veins) are especially dangerous.

  19. Solid organs with a single arterial supply, such as the heart, spleen and kidney, mainly develop red (hemorrhagic) infarcts.

    X · False Solid organs with a single arterial supply develop white (anemic) infarcts. They are wedge-shaped, with the apex pointing toward the occluded vessel.

  20. In early septic shock, peripheral vasodilation may make the skin warm and flushed.

    O · True A systemic inflammatory response to agents such as gram-negative endotoxin (LPS) dilates the vessels (warm shock).

  21. The tuberculin (PPD) reaction is an immediate reaction that peaks within 20–30 minutes after injection.

    X · False The tuberculin reaction is a T cell–mediated delayed-type (type IV) hypersensitivity that peaks at 48–72 hours.

  22. Amyloid stained with Congo red shows apple-green birefringence under polarized light.

    O · True Amyloid consists of protein fibrils in a β-pleated sheet structure.

  23. A lipoma is a malignant tumor of adipose tissue.

    X · False A lipoma is benign. The malignant tumor of adipose tissue is liposarcoma.

  24. Melanoma and lymphoma end in "-oma" but are malignant tumors.

    O · True Seminoma and mesothelioma are also malignant despite their names.

  25. The characteristic translocation of Burkitt lymphoma is t(14;18), involving the BCL2 gene.

    X · False Burkitt lymphoma has t(8;14) involving MYC. t(14;18) and BCL2 are characteristic of follicular lymphoma.

Studying with true/false statements

When you get one wrong, read the explanation and say the corrected statement to yourself. Many false statements swap one term of a confusing pair, so fixing them sharpens exactly those distinctions.
