Pharmacology: True or False Questions

Med school pharmacology as true/false statements, each with a short explanation. Decide first, then tap a statement to check.

Pharmacology is mostly drug names attached to mechanisms, side effects and antidotes, and a wrong pairing can look perfectly reasonable. True/false statements make you check each pairing instead of just recognizing the names.

The questions follow the usual order of a pharmacology course: pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, autonomic drugs, cardiovascular, renal and blood drugs, CNS drugs, antimicrobials and antivirals, anticancer and immunosuppressive drugs, endocrine and anti-inflammatory drugs, and antidotes.

25 practice questions

These are 25 of the 100 questions in the app’s example deck, spread across all topics.

  1. The reduction of an oral drug by hepatic metabolism before it reaches the systemic circulation is called the first-pass effect.

    O · True Nitroglycerin is given sublingually to avoid the first-pass effect.

  2. A drug with a large volume of distribution (Vd) stays mostly in the plasma.

    X · False A drug with a large Vd distributes widely into tissues. A drug with a small Vd stays mainly in the plasma.

  3. With repeated dosing at the same interval, increasing each dose shortens the time to reach steady state.

    X · False The time to steady state is about 4–5 half-lives regardless of dose. The dose changes the steady-state level.

  4. Glucuronidation is a phase I reaction carried out by cytochrome P450.

    X · False Glucuronidation is a phase II reaction carried out by UDP-glucuronosyltransferases (UGT). Phase II reactions make drugs more water-soluble and help excretion.

  5. In poisoning with weak acids such as aspirin, alkalinizing the urine increases their excretion.

    O · True Ionized drug in the urine cannot be reabsorbed by the tubules (ion trapping).

  6. Efficacy is the dose needed to produce a given effect, and potency is the maximal effect a drug can produce.

    X · False It is the reverse. Efficacy is the maximal effect (Emax); potency is the dose needed for a given effect (the lower the EC50, the higher the potency).

  7. The insulin receptor is a G protein–coupled receptor.

    X · False The insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase.

  8. When the combined effect of two drugs is greater than the sum of their separate effects, it is called synergism.

    O · True Example: trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole.

  9. Metoprolol and atenolol are β1-selective blockers.

    O · True They are cardioselective, but selectivity decreases at high doses.

  10. Clonidine is an α2 receptor antagonist that increases sympathetic activity.

    X · False Clonidine is a central α2 agonist that reduces sympathetic outflow and lowers blood pressure. Stopping it suddenly can cause rebound hypertension.

  11. Succinylcholine is a nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker.

    X · False Succinylcholine is a depolarizing neuromuscular blocker and can cause hyperkalemia and malignant hyperthermia. Rocuronium and similar drugs are nondepolarizing.

  12. Dry cough, a typical side effect of ACE inhibitors, is caused by accumulation of bradykinin.

    O · True Angioedema can occur by the same mechanism. If the cough is troublesome, switch to an ARB.

  13. Thiazide diuretics lower blood calcium and are therefore used to treat hypercalcemia.

    X · False Thiazides increase calcium reabsorption and raise blood calcium (so they are used to prevent calcium stones). They can also cause hyperglycemia and hyperuricemia.

  14. Hypokalemia reduces digoxin toxicity.

    X · False K⁺ and digoxin compete for the same site on the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, so hypokalemia increases digoxin toxicity.

  15. Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and lower LDL cholesterol.

    O · True Hepatic LDL receptors increase. Side effects include myopathy and elevated liver enzymes.

  16. Opioid poisoning is characterized by dilated pupils (mydriasis).

    X · False Opioid poisoning is characterized by pinpoint pupils (miosis), respiratory depression and decreased consciousness. It is treated with naloxone.

  17. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) usually take several weeks to show an antidepressant effect.

    O · True Because the effect is delayed, patients should be told not to stop early.

  18. Levodopa is used instead of dopamine in Parkinson disease because dopamine cannot cross the blood–brain barrier.

    O · True Carbidopa is given with it to stop levodopa from being converted to dopamine in the periphery.

  19. Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) can be treated with penicillin if a β-lactamase inhibitor is added.

    X · False MRSA has an altered PBP (PBP2a) and is resistant to most β-lactams. Vancomycin, linezolid and others are used.

  20. Tetracyclines are avoided in children under 8 and in pregnant women because they discolor teeth.

    O · True Taking them with calcium, antacids or iron reduces absorption (chelation).

  21. Peripheral neuropathy caused by isoniazid is prevented by giving vitamin B6 (pyridoxine).

    O · True Hepatotoxicity of isoniazid is also a concern.

  22. Rifampin inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase.

    X · False Rifampin inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase. It can turn urine, tears and other fluids orange.

  23. Cyclosporine and tacrolimus inhibit calcineurin and reduce IL-2 production by T cells.

    O · True They are used to prevent rejection after organ transplantation. Nephrotoxicity is the typical side effect.

  24. Metformin lowers blood glucose by increasing insulin secretion, so it often causes hypoglycemia even when used alone.

    X · False Metformin mainly reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis, and hypoglycemia is rare when it is used alone. Lactic acidosis is a rare concern.

  25. Stopping long-term glucocorticoids suddenly can cause adrenal insufficiency.

    O · True The hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis is suppressed, so the dose is tapered gradually.

Studying with true/false statements

When you get one wrong, read the explanation and say the corrected statement to yourself. Many false statements attach the right effect to the wrong drug, which is exactly the kind of mistake that costs points in exams.
