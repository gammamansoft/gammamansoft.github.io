Medical Physiology: True or False Questions

Med school physiology as true/false statements, each with a short explanation. Decide first, then tap a statement to check.

Physiology is full of directions and numbers that are easy to flip: which ion goes in and which goes out, what rises and what falls, which segment of the nephron does what. A true/false statement makes you commit to one answer, so a half-remembered fact shows up right away.

The questions follow the usual order of a physiology course: membranes and membrane potentials, nerve and muscle, cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, body fluids and acid–base, gastrointestinal, and endocrine and reproductive physiology.

25 practice questions

These are 25 of the 100 questions in the app’s example deck, spread across all topics.

  1. Intracellular fluid is rich in K⁺, and extracellular fluid is rich in Na⁺.

    O · True This concentration difference is maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase.

  2. For each ATP, the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps 2 Na⁺ out of the cell and 3 K⁺ into it.

    X · False It pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in. One positive charge leaves per cycle, so it is an electrogenic pump.

  3. When extracellular K⁺ concentration rises, the resting membrane potential becomes more negative (hyperpolarization).

    X · False The K⁺ gradient shrinks, so the membrane depolarizes. This is why hyperkalemia causes dangerous arrhythmias.

  4. In myelinated fibers, the action potential jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next (saltatory conduction), so conduction is fast.

    O · True When myelin is damaged, as in multiple sclerosis, conduction slows or is blocked.

  5. Myasthenia gravis is caused by autoantibodies against voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the nerve terminal.

    X · False Myasthenia gravis is caused by autoantibodies against nicotinic acetylcholine receptors. Antibodies against Ca²⁺ channels cause Lambert-Eaton syndrome.

  6. In skeletal muscle, DHP receptors in the T tubules mechanically open ryanodine receptors of the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release Ca²⁺.

    O · True In cardiac muscle, Ca²⁺ entering through DHP receptors (L-type Ca²⁺ channels) opens ryanodine receptors (Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release).

  7. Muscle spindles detect muscle tension, and Golgi tendon organs detect changes in muscle length.

    X · False It is the reverse. Muscle spindles detect muscle length (and its rate of change), and Golgi tendon organs detect muscle tension.

  8. The normal pacemaker of the heart is the atrioventricular (AV) node.

    X · False The normal pacemaker is the sinoatrial (SA) node, which has the fastest rate of spontaneous depolarization.

  9. The upstroke (phase 0) of the SA node action potential is caused mainly by Ca²⁺ influx.

    O · True It is carried by L-type Ca²⁺ channels. The funny current (If) contributes to spontaneous depolarization in phase 4.

  10. The first heart sound is produced by closure of the atrioventricular valves (mitral and tricuspid).

    O · True The second heart sound is produced by closure of the semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonary).

  11. The murmur of aortic stenosis is a diastolic murmur.

    X · False Aortic stenosis causes a systolic ejection murmur. Diastolic murmurs are heard in conditions such as aortic regurgitation.

  12. Vital capacity is the volume exhaled after a maximal inspiration and includes the residual volume.

    X · False Vital capacity does not include the residual volume. Total lung capacity = vital capacity + residual volume.

  13. Residual volume cannot be measured directly by ordinary spirometry.

    O · True It is measured by helium dilution, nitrogen washout or body plethysmography.

  14. When alveolar oxygen pressure falls, the pulmonary vessels in that area dilate.

    X · False Pulmonary vessels constrict in hypoxia (hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction), diverting blood away from poorly ventilated areas to match V/Q — the opposite of systemic vessels.

  15. Carbon monoxide binds hemoglobin more than 200 times more strongly than oxygen does.

    O · True It reduces oxygen-carrying capacity and shifts the dissociation curve to the left, so less oxygen is released to tissues.

  16. Constriction of the afferent arteriole decreases the glomerular filtration rate.

    O · True It lowers the hydrostatic pressure in the glomerular capillaries.

  17. Most (about two-thirds) of the filtered Na⁺ is reabsorbed in the collecting duct.

    X · False About two-thirds is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule. The collecting duct reabsorbs only a small amount but fine-tunes it under aldosterone.

  18. Renin is secreted by the juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney.

    O · True Decreased renal perfusion pressure, sympathetic (β1) stimulation and decreased NaCl delivery to the macula densa increase its secretion.

  19. In metabolic acidosis, breathing compensates by becoming slow and shallow, so PCO2 rises.

    X · False In metabolic acidosis, breathing becomes fast and deep (Kussmaul breathing), lowering PCO2 to compensate.

  20. Secretin is released when acid enters the duodenum and stimulates the pancreas to secrete bicarbonate-rich fluid.

    O · True It is secreted by duodenal S cells. Neutralizing gastric acid lets pancreatic enzymes work.

  21. Vitamin B12 is absorbed mainly in the jejunum.

    X · False Vitamin B12 is absorbed bound to intrinsic factor in the terminal ileum.

  22. Steroid hormones are lipid-soluble and usually bind intracellular receptors to regulate gene transcription.

    O · True Peptide hormones usually bind cell membrane receptors and act through second messengers.

  23. Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) is secreted by the hypothalamus.

    X · False TSH is secreted by the anterior pituitary. The hypothalamus secretes thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH).

  24. Calcitonin is secreted by the parafollicular (C) cells of the thyroid and raises blood calcium.

    X · False Calcitonin inhibits osteoclasts and lowers blood calcium. Its role in humans is minor.

  25. The most important stimuli for aldosterone secretion are angiotensin II and increased plasma K⁺.

    O · True ACTH also increases secretion, but its effect is small.

Studying with true/false statements

When you get one wrong, read the explanation and say the corrected statement to yourself. False statements are written to look like common mistakes, so fixing them is where most of the learning happens.
