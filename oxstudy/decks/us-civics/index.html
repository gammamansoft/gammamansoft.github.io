U.S. Citizenship Civics Test: True or False Practice

20 true/false statements based on the official USCIS civics questions (2025 version). Decide whether each one is true or false, then tap it to check the answer.

In the naturalization interview, a USCIS officer asks you civics questions about American government and history out loud. The 2025 version of the test uses a list of 128 questions: the officer asks up to 20 of them, and you need 12 correct answers to pass.

Turning a question into a true or false statement is a quick way to check what you really remember. A wrong number or a wrong name sounds right when you only reread the answer list, but it is much harder to miss when you have to say “true” or “false” yourself.

20 practice questions

  1. The U.S. Constitution is the supreme law of the land.

    O · True The Constitution sets up and defines the government and protects the basic rights of Americans.

  2. The U.S. Constitution has 10 amendments.

    X · False It has 27 amendments. The first ten are called the Bill of Rights.

  3. The U.S. Constitution was written in 1776.

    X · False The Constitution was written in 1787. The Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776.

  4. George Washington wrote the Declaration of Independence.

    X · False Thomas Jefferson wrote it.

  5. The three branches of government are the legislative, executive, and judicial branches.

    O · True Congress, the president, and the courts.

  6. The two parts of the U.S. Congress are the Senate and the House of Representatives.

    O · True Together they form Congress, the legislative branch, which makes federal laws.

  7. There are 50 U.S. senators.

    X · False There are 100 senators – two from each state.

  8. A U.S. senator is elected for a term of six years.

    O · True Senate terms are staggered, so about one third of the Senate is up for election every two years.

  9. The House of Representatives has 435 voting members.

    O · True The number of representatives from each state depends on the state’s population.

  10. A member of the House of Representatives serves a four-year term.

    X · False A term in the House is two years.

  11. The President of the United States is elected for four years.

    O · True Under the 22nd Amendment, a person can be elected president no more than twice.

  12. The Vice President is Commander in Chief of the U.S. military.

    X · False The President is Commander in Chief.

  13. The President signs bills to become laws and can veto bills.

    O · True Congress can override a veto with a two-thirds vote in both the Senate and the House.

  14. There are nine seats on the Supreme Court.

    O · True The Court has one Chief Justice and eight associate justices.

  15. Supreme Court justices serve ten-year terms.

    X · False They serve for life (until retirement).

  16. U.S. senators are appointed by the governor of their state.

    X · False Citizens from their state elect them.

  17. The United States bought the Louisiana Territory from Spain in 1803.

    X · False It was bought from France.

  18. Thomas Jefferson issued the Emancipation Proclamation, which freed the slaves.

    X · False Abraham Lincoln, the 16th president, issued it during the Civil War.

  19. The capital of the United States is New York City.

    X · False The capital is Washington, D.C. The Statue of Liberty is in New York Harbor.

  20. The national anthem is "America the Beautiful."

    X · False The national anthem is "The Star-Spangled Banner."

How to study for the real test

This page is a trial set of 20 statements. Study the full official list of 128 questions and answers from USCIS as well, because the officer can ask any of them.

Some answers change with elections, such as the names of your senators, your U.S. representative, the governor of your state, and the current President and Vice President. Check them shortly before your interview.

The test is spoken, not written. Practice answering out loud.
