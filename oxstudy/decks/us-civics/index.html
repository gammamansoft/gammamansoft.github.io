U.S. Citizenship Civics Test: True or False Practice

30 true/false statements based on the official USCIS civics questions (2025 version), from every part of the list. Decide whether each one is true or false, then tap it to check the answer.

In the naturalization interview, a USCIS officer asks you civics questions about American government and history out loud. The 2025 version of the test uses a list of 128 questions: the officer asks up to 20 of them, and you need 12 correct answers to pass.

Turning a question into a true or false statement is a quick way to check what you really remember. A wrong number or a wrong name sounds right when you only reread the answer list, but it is much harder to miss when you have to say “true” or “false” yourself.

30 practice questions

These are 30 of the 120 questions in the app’s example deck, spread across all topics.

  1. The form of government of the United States is a republic.

    O · True It is a constitution-based federal republic and a representative democracy. (Question 1)

  2. The U.S. Constitution only protects the rights of the government.

    X · False It forms the government, defines its powers and parts, and protects the rights of the people. (Question 3)

  3. The Declaration of Independence says that all people are created equal.

    O · True It also says America is free from British control and identifies inherent rights. (Question 8)

  4. The U.S. Constitution was the founding document that said the American colonies were free from Britain.

    X · False That was the Declaration of Independence. (Question 9)

  5. There are three branches of government so that one part does not become too powerful.

    O · True This is called checks and balances, or separation of powers. (Question 15)

  6. The Supreme Court is the part of the federal government that writes laws.

    X · False Congress (the legislative branch) writes laws. (Question 18)

  7. The House of Representatives has 435 voting members.

    O · True The number of representatives from each state depends on its population. (Question 24)

  8. A U.S. senator represents only the people who voted for them.

    X · False A senator represents all the citizens (people) of their state. (Question 31)

  9. Some states have more representatives than others because they have more people.

    O · True Representation in the House is based on each state's population. (Question 35)

  10. The President can serve only two terms because of the 1st Amendment.

    X · False It is because of the 22nd Amendment, to keep the president from becoming too powerful. (Question 37)

  11. The President signs bills to become laws and can veto bills.

    O · True The official answer to both questions is the President (of the United States). (Questions 43 and 44)

  12. The President's Cabinet writes the laws of the United States.

    X · False The Cabinet advises the President; Congress writes laws. (Question 47)

  13. There are nine seats on the Supreme Court.

    O · True The official answer is nine (9). (Question 53)

  14. Supreme Court justices serve ten-year terms.

    X · False They serve for life (until retirement). (Question 55)

  15. One of the voting amendments says that citizens eighteen and older can vote.

    O · True Other voting amendments ended the poll tax, gave women the vote, and gave men of any race the vote. (Question 63)

  16. In the Oath of Allegiance, new citizens promise to keep their loyalty to their former countries.

    X · False They promise to give up loyalty to other countries, defend the Constitution, and obey U.S. laws. (Question 67)

  17. Voting and joining a political party are examples of civic participation.

    O · True Others are running for office, helping with a campaign, and contacting elected officials. (Question 69)

  18. Americans won independence from Britain in the Civil War.

    X · False They fought the American Revolution (Revolutionary War). The Civil War was between the North and the South. (Question 76)

  19. The Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776.

    O · True The official answer is July 4, 1776. (Question 79)

  20. The Federalist Papers argued against passing the U.S. Constitution.

    X · False They supported passing it and helped people understand it. (Question 84)

  21. George Washington was the first president of the United States.

    O · True He is called the "Father of Our Country." (Question 86)

  22. The United States bought the Louisiana Territory from Spain in 1803.

    X · False It was bought from France. (Question 90)

  23. Abraham Lincoln was the 16th president of the United States.

    O · True He led the country during the Civil War and saved the Union. (Question 94)

  24. All men got the right to vote in 1776.

    X · False They got it after the Civil War, with the 15th Amendment (1870). (Question 98)

  25. Franklin Roosevelt was president during the Great Depression and World War II.

    O · True The official answer is (Franklin) Roosevelt. (Question 105)

  26. Dwight Eisenhower was a general during the Civil War.

    X · False He was a general in World War II and later the 34th president. (Question 107)

  27. The United States entered the Vietnam War to stop the spread of communism.

    O · True The official answer is "to stop the spread of communism." (Question 111)

  28. The United States entered the Persian Gulf War to force the Soviet military out of Afghanistan.

    X · False It entered to force the Iraqi military from Kuwait. (Question 114)

  29. The light bulb, the airplane, and the moon landing are examples of American innovation.

    O · True Others are the automobile, skyscrapers, the assembly line, and the integrated circuit. (Question 118)

  30. The flag has 50 stars because there were 50 signers of the Declaration of Independence.

    X · False There is one star for each of the 50 states. (Question 122)

How to study for the real test

This page shows 30 statements. The app’s example deck has 120: one for each question on the official list, in the same order, leaving out the questions whose answers depend on where you live or on who holds office now. Study the full official list of 128 questions and answers from USCIS as well, because the officer can ask any of them.

Some answers change with elections, such as the names of your senators, your U.S. representative, the governor of your state, and the current President and Vice President. Check them shortly before your interview.

The test is spoken, not written. Practice answering out loud.
